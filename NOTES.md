# Notes — The Houseplant Out-of-Office

Final line (103 characters):

> Auto-reply: last watered in March, still alive, still judging you. Your 'urgent' email can wait. — Fern

Why it won: the plant's own life drives the joke — months of neglect,
survival anyway, and payback by ignoring the sender back. "Still judging
you" is the surprise turn: the fern is not a victim asking for water, it
is a witness with opinions. Short, no wasted words, no office cliche.

## Discarded alternatives

1. "Out of office: please water me, I'm literally dying. — Your fern"
   (66 chars) — Rejected: a plea for water is the most obvious houseplant
   line; passive victim, no surprise, and "literally dying" is worn out.
2. "Gone to photosynthesize. Back when the sun is. — Monstera"
   (61 chars) — Rejected: a mild pun, and the judge brief explicitly
   ranks a fresh surprising joke above a mild pun. Photosynthesis is
   also the first word every plant joke reaches for.
3. "Out of office and out of water. This is fine. — Cactus"
   (57 chars) — Rejected: "out of office / out of water" is a stock
   parallel, and "This is fine" borrows a meme instead of being original.
4. "I thrive on neglect, so your email can wait until 2027. — Cactus"
   (65 chars) — Rejected: "thriving on neglect" is the cliche every
   cactus joke uses, and the 2027 date is a gimmick that spends
   characters without adding a joke.
5. "The monstera has opinions about this window and will not be taking
   questions. — Management" (88 chars) — Rejected: funny premise, but
   the joke is about the window, not the plant's neglected life, and
   "will not be taking questions" is an office-press-conference cliche.
6. "Auto-reply: I'm a fern. I was ignored since March, so I'm ignoring
   you back." (74 chars) — Rejected: closest to the final line, but
   "ignoring you back" only mirrors the neglect; "still judging you"
   escalates it into a verdict, which is funnier and more surprising.
